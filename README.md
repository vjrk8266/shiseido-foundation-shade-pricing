## What each formula actually is

**Synchro Skin Radiant Lifting** is the one to look at if you want visible coverage and don't mind a glow. It's medium-to-full and buildable, so you can stop at one layer or keep going. The technology Shiseido markets here is Light-Adjusting: radiant microcrystals that soften the look of fine lines, a luminous pearl that visually counters uneven tone, and a diffusing matrix that adds depth. Argan oil handles the hydration, barium sulphate addresses roughness, and star fruit leaf plus beech tree bud extracts are there to support firmness over time. Transfer-resistant, water-resistant, non-comedogenic, dermatologist-tested.

**Synchro Skin Self-Refreshing** goes the other direction. The finish is fresh and natural rather than luminous, the coverage is medium and buildable, and the whole pitch is climate resistance. ActiveForce technology is designed to keep the base looking just-applied through heat, sweat and movement, and the formula is waterproof, smudge-proof, humidity-resistant and transfer-resistant. It's also fragrance-free, which matters if you react to scented base products. For anyone in a humid city, this is usually the more practical pick of the two.

**RevitalEssence Skin Glow** is the skincare-first option. It's a serum-like liquid that lands at medium, buildable coverage with a glow finish, and it wears 12 hours while hydrating for 24. The two headline ingredients are fermented kefir+ and niacinamide. Shiseido's own consumer testing claims 98% agreed it glides on weightlessly, 96% said skin looked instantly more even-toned, and 93% said the finish looked like real skin; at one week, 94% reported smoother bare skin and 86% saw less visible lines. Those are brand-run panels, so read them as directions of travel rather than proof. The bottle is 30% recycled glass, and the formula is fragrance-free, paraben-free and mineral-oil free.

**The two compacts** are for different jobs. The Synchro Skin Self-Refreshing Custom Finish Powder Foundation is a buildable matte powder you can use wet or dry, which makes it work as both a light base and a touch-up. The UV Protective Compact Foundation is the SPF-first option, formulated with SuperVeil-UV360 and safflower extract, sweat- and sebum-resistant, and refillable, so the second purchase costs less than the first.

## Matching the formula to your skin

Dry or mature skin, or a preference for a lit-from-within look: Radiant Lifting. The argan oil and the luminous finish both work in your favour, and the fine-line diffusion is the specific problem it was built around.

Oily, combination, or living somewhere with real humidity: Self-Refreshing. Fresh rather than dewy, resistance to heat and sweat, fragrance-free.

Minimal-makeup days, or skin that breaks out when you layer too much: RevitalEssence. Medium coverage that reads like skin, and two prebiotics in the formula instead of heavy occlusives.

Touch-ups, oily afternoons, or a base you can control in layers: the Custom Finish powder.

Sun exposure as the main concern: the UV Protective Compact.

One more thing worth knowing: on the Japanese official store page, Radiant Lifting shows an average rating of 4.1 from 7 reviews. Small sample, and it's the brand's own site, but the reviews are specific. One buyer on their fourth bottle described the coverage as high but not cakey. Another on their second bottle mentioned noticing only later why "lifting" is in the name. A third, shopping in the 330 shade, noted coverage and wear time as the reasons they switched. Not a consensus, just honest texture.

## Shade numbers, and how far you can trust them

Shiseido shade names follow a nature-and-material theme: Opal, Porcelain, Shell, Linen, Alder, Quartz, Sand, Cashmere, Silk, Pine, Bamboo, Oak, Citrine, Sunstone, Copper, Topaz, Suede. The numbering climbs from the 100s to the 500s, and some listings carry a letter suffix such as the 140N shade stocked by retailers.

Here's the part that trips people up. The range isn't consistent across markets or formulas, so you cannot map a shade name from one foundation to another with confidence. A 160 Shell in the Japanese Radiant Lifting lineup and a 160 Shell elsewhere may not be the same colour. Ordering blind across borders is where returns happen.

The practical workaround is to decide your shade on the formula you've settled on, in the market you're buying from, and to check whether the retailer you're using accepts shade returns. If you're between two shades and your skin has strong yellow or pink undertones, the lighter of the two is usually the safer error for a radiant finish, because a luminous formula amplifies whatever it's sitting on.
